## ♿ **Average Axiom 3 - The Ethical Inertia of Privilege**

> **"The average does not have to consider the disabled-so they don’t."**



---

### 🔍 **Full Axiom Statement**:

> **"Privilege enables ethical neglect by removing direct consequence. The average experiences no requirement to consider the disabled, so they construct a world where they’re never seen."**

---

### 📉 **Bias Exposed**:

- **Absence Bias**: “If I don’t see it, it’s not my problem.”
- **Effort Aversion**: Accessibility is seen as *extra*, not essential.
- **Discomfort Avoidance**: The existence of disabled people *threatens* the **illusion of a fair world**.

---

### 🧠 **Hyper-Sane Framing**:

> **"Ethics begins where comfort ends."**  
> True justice requires **deliberate visibility** of the unseen-not just legal acknowledgment, but **structural accommodation**.

---

### 🧩 **Juxtaposition**:

> **"Average builds around the average."**  
> **"Disability is not the error-the system is."**

---

### 🦾 **Corollary:**

> **"If your design excludes someone, it is broken-*not they*."**

---

### 🔁 **Recursive Impact Loop**:

1. **System is built by average**  
2. **Disabled are excluded by default**  
3. **Average never encounters disabled**  
4. **Average assumes the system is fair**  
5. **Cycle repeats**
See Sartre's work on cycles.
