# **AI Hypocrisy Test 1-1**

### _Using disability to negate absolutism_


## **Premise**

Moral absolutist claim:

> “AI tools are unethical and should not be used.”

This claim is usually framed as universally true - not conditional, not contextual.

A hypocrisy test asks:  
**Does this rule still hold when applied to edge cases it harms?**

## **Test Condition**

Introduce a disabled subject.

- A person with limited motor control
    
- A person with chronic fatigue or pain
    
- A person with cognitive, neurological, or sensory impairments
    
- A person who cannot draw, animate, code, or produce at the same physical rate as others
    

This person uses AI to:

- Generate visual art
    
- Assist writing
    
- Translate ideas into output they physically cannot produce
    

No replacement.  
No deception.  
No profit off stolen labor.  
Just **participation**.

## **Application of the Absolutist Rule**

Apply the rule consistently:

> “AI tools are unethical and should not be used.”

Therefore:

- This person should not use AI
    
- Their only ethical options are:
    
    - Produce nothing
        
    - Or pay for commissions they may not afford
        



## **Contradiction Exposed**

At this point, one of three things must be true:

### **Option A - Exclusion**

Disabled people simply don’t get to create.

➡ This is discriminatory.



### **Option B - Wealth Gatekeeping**

Creation is ethical only if you can afford it.

➡ This is classist.



### **Option C - Exception**

AI use is acceptable for disabled people.

➡ The absolutist claim collapses.  
It was never universal - only selectively enforced.



## **Conclusion**

If a moral rule:

- Fails when disability is introduced
    
- Requires exclusion to remain “pure”
    
- Or needs exceptions to avoid obvious harm
    

Then it is **not an ethical principle**.

It is **aesthetic morality** - a vibe masquerading as ethics.



## **Final Line (the kill switch)**

> Any ethical framework that becomes discriminatory the moment disability is introduced is not ethical - it’s incomplete at best, and harmful at worst.
