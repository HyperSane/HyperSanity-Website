# Ignoration:

> [!abstract]+
> 
> - `Ignoration`: Dismissive, bad-faith engagement that lacks logic or reason.
>     
> - `Often By Choice`: Refusal to practice due diligence, engage relevant evidence, or seriously examine information that threatens a preferred belief, identity, behavior, or conclusion.
>     
> - `Epistemic Refusal`: Ignoration is not merely _not knowing_, but the active or motivated preservation of not-knowing when greater understanding would threaten an existing psychological, ideological, social, or moral position.
>     

**Ignoration** (**noun**): A self-perpetuating cycle of ignorance in which an individual resists or rejects relevant knowledge, empiricism, reasoning, or consequences, then distorts or reframes truth in order to protect an existing belief, identity, behavior, status, or desired conclusion. This can include weaponizing relativism, skepticism, ambiguity, or pseudo-philosophical reasoning to evade correction, destabilize others, preserve perceived superiority, or avoid accountability.

Ignoration differs from ordinary **ignorance**:

> **Ignorance:** _I do not know._

> **Ignoration:** _I do not want this knowledge to become operative because accepting it may require me to revise what I believe, what I am doing, or how I understand myself._

This distinction has a philosophical precursor in Friedrich Nietzsche's observation that **“Faith means not wanting to know what is true.”** The relevance to Ignoration is not that all faith automatically constitutes Ignoration, but that Nietzsche identifies its central epistemic mechanism: **the will can participate in preserving ignorance.**

Ignoration therefore occurs when an unknown is no longer merely absent information, but becomes something the individual has a psychological, ideological, or practical incentive **not to resolve honestly**.

## Ignoration Cycle

**Existing belief / identity / behavior**

↓

**Relevant evidence, contradiction, criticism, or consequence appears**

↓

**Acceptance would create cognitive, social, ethical, or behavioral cost**

↓

**Motivated refusal to seriously engage**

↓

**Dismissal / distortion / relativization / rationalization**

↓

**Existing model remains protected**

↓

**Corrective information becomes increasingly threatening**

↓

**Further Ignoration**

The cycle becomes self-perpetuating because every successful act of avoidance protects not only the original conclusion, but also the person's previous decision to reject correction.

## Key Features

### Epistemic Refusal

The defining transition from ignorance into Ignoration occurs when a person has meaningful access to corrective information but develops resistance to knowing, investigating, or allowing that information to alter their conclusions.

The relevant question is therefore not only:

> **“Does this person know?”**

but also:

> **“What happens when they are given a reasonable opportunity to know?”**

---

### Empiricidal Tendencies

[[Hyper-Sanity/HS-Psychology/Hypo-Psychology/Ignoration/Empiricidal Tendencies|Empiricidal Tendencies]]

The outright murder of facts by **misusing philosophical principles such as relativism and skepticism**.

Rather than using skepticism to improve epistemic accuracy, the individual uses skepticism selectively to make inconvenient evidence impossible to establish.

This creates an asymmetry:

> **Evidence supporting the preferred conclusion is considered sufficient.**

> **Evidence threatening the preferred conclusion is subjected to unlimited doubt.**

---

### Pseudo-Philosophical Manipulation

[[Pseudo-Philosophical Manipulation]]

Statements such as:

- “Facts aren't facts.”
    
- “Truth is relative.”
    
- “That's just your interpretation.”
    
- “Nobody can really know anything.”
    

can become shields against accountability when they are not consistently applied as genuine philosophical positions, but selectively introduced whenever empirical or logical scrutiny threatens a desired conclusion.

The philosophical concept is therefore being used **instrumentally rather than philosophically**.

---

### Corrective Resistance

Ignoration is distinguished not merely by holding an incorrect belief, but by the person's relationship to correction.

A mistaken person can encounter evidence and revise.

Ignoration instead converts correction itself into a threat.

**Correction → identity threat → defensive reasoning → dismissal → greater commitment**

This is why Ignoration can paradoxically intensify as contrary evidence becomes stronger.

---

### Consequence Avoidance

Some truths imply behavioral obligations.

A person may resist a factual conclusion not because the evidence is especially difficult to understand, but because accepting it would force another question:

> **“If this is true, what would I now have to change?”**

This produces:

**Truth accepted → behavior questioned**

so the defensive solution becomes:

**Truth rejected → behavior preserved**

Ignoration therefore has an ethical dimension whenever refusing relevant knowledge permits avoidable harm to continue.

---

### Cyclic and Abusive

Like a snake eating its tail, Ignoration can loop back upon itself, resisting correction and punishing those who attempt to introduce corrective information.

In interpersonal contexts this may become abusive when the individual moves beyond defending their own belief and instead attempts to destabilize another person's confidence in demonstrable reality.

The sequence can become:

**ignorance**

→ **correction offered**

→ **correction rejected**

→ **truth distorted**

→ **corrector attacked**

→ **distortion socially reinforced**

→ **original ignorance becomes harder to escape**

Ignoration therefore describes not merely an absence of knowledge, but a **maintenance system for ignorance**.

## HyperSanity Distinction

> **Ignorance is an epistemic condition.  
> Ignoration is an epistemic behavior.**

And, in its strongest form:

> **Ignoration is the conversion of “I do not know” into “I must prevent this from becoming something I know.”**

The HyperSanity response is not to demand certainty, but to preserve the ability for reality to alter the model:

**uncertainty → investigation → evidence → revision**

rather than:

**threat → dismissal → rationalization → preserved ignorance**
