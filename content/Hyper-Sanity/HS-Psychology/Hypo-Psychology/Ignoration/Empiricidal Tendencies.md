# Empiricidal Tendencies

> [!abstract]+ Definition  
> **Empiricidal Tendencies** are recurring epistemic behaviors that function to **kill, disable, or neutralize the corrective force of empirical evidence** by denying, distorting, relativizing, selectively demanding, suppressing, or reclassifying evidence whenever it threatens a preferred belief, identity, narrative, authority, or conclusion.
> 
> **Short form:**  
> **Error is not empiricidal. Making correction impossible is.**

The **-cidal** in _Empiricidal_ is metaphorical. It refers not to physical violence, but to the destruction of the epistemic conditions through which claims can be tested against reality.

The original Hyper-Sanity formulation described it as:

> **The outright murder of facts through the misuse of philosophical principles such as relativism and skepticism.**

The expanded concept is broader.

A person does not need to literally deny every fact to display an Empiricidal Tendency. The defining feature is that **evidence loses its legitimate power to change the conclusion**.

# Core Structure

The distinction can be reduced to two opposite epistemic directions.

### Empirical cognition

**Evidence → constrains belief**

The observer asks:

> What conclusion is best supported by what can actually be demonstrated?

Beliefs remain provisional because new evidence may require revision.

### Empiricidal cognition

**Belief → determines whether evidence is allowed to count**

The observer begins with a preferred conclusion and then decides which observations, sources, methods, or standards will be permitted to threaten it.

The inversion is fundamental:

> **Empiricism modifies the model to survive reality.**  
> **Empiricidal cognition modifies reality's admissibility to preserve the model.**

---

# Empiricism vs Empiricide

Empiricism does **not** mean:

- Every current scientific claim is permanently correct.
    
- Every measurement is flawless.
    
- Consensus is infallible.
    
- Numbers interpret themselves.
    
- Skepticism is forbidden.
    
- A claim becomes true merely because somebody calls it a fact.
    

Those would themselves be anti-empirical positions.

Empiricism requires:

**observation → testing → criticism → uncertainty → replication → correction → revision**

Its strength is precisely that conclusions can change.

Empiricidal reasoning instead attacks that corrective process when correction becomes psychologically, socially, ideologically, or personally costly.

> [!important]+ Critical Distinction  
> **Questioning evidence is not Empiricidal.**
> 
> Genuine skepticism asks whether evidence is strong enough.
> 
> Empiricidal skepticism constructs standards under which **disfavored evidence can never become strong enough**, while favored claims are accepted under weaker standards.

---

# What Empiricidal Tendencies Are Not

|Behavior|Empiricidal?|Reason|
|---|---|---|
|Not knowing something|No|This is ordinary ignorance.|
|Making a factual mistake|No|Error remains compatible with correction.|
|Changing your mind|No|This is evidence-responsive cognition.|
|Questioning a study's methodology|No|Methodological criticism is part of empiricism.|
|Remaining uncertain|No|Uncertainty can be epistemically responsible.|
|Disagreeing with consensus|Not inherently|Consensus can be challenged through evidence and reasoning.|
|Applying extreme skepticism only to inconvenient evidence|**Potentially**|Evidential standards become asymmetric.|
|Calling measurable evidence “just opinion” solely because it conflicts with a belief|**Yes, as a tendency**|The fact/belief distinction is deliberately or defensively collapsed.|
|Making a claim impossible to falsify|**Potentially**|Evidence has been stripped of corrective power.|
|Changing standards every time counterevidence satisfies the previous standard|**Yes**|The goal has shifted from testing the belief to protecting it.|

The concept therefore identifies a **pattern**, not an ontological class of people.

Someone can display an Empiricidal Tendency in one domain while reasoning empirically in another.

# The Empiricidal Loop

A common sequence is:

**preferred belief / identity / narrative**

↓

**disconfirming evidence appears**

↓

**evidence creates cognitive or identity threat**

↓

**epistemic defense activates**

↓

**evidence is denied, relativized, reclassified, attacked, or subjected to a new standard**

↓

**original belief survives**

↓

**survival of the belief feels like confirmation**

↓

**future evidence is filtered through the preserved model**

↓

**correction becomes progressively harder**

This can eventually feed directly into [[Ignoration]].

> **Evidence does not merely fail to correct the model.**  
> **The failure of correction becomes part of the mechanism protecting the model.**

---

# Major Forms

## 1. Empirical Nullification

An empirical claim is stripped of factual status without a methodological reason.

Examples:

> “That's just your opinion.”

> “Those are your facts.”

The important question is whether the disputed claim is actually subjective.

Preferences may legitimately differ.

Measurements do not become preferences merely because their implications are uncomfortable.

---

## 2. Relativist Weaponization

Philosophical relativism is taken outside the domain where it is useful and employed to erase distinctions between:

**perspective**

and

**external reality**

For example:

> “Everyone has their own truth.”

This may make sense when discussing personal meaning or subjective experience.

It becomes Empiricidal when used to imply that mutually incompatible empirical claims are simultaneously true merely because different people believe them.

---

## 3. Asymmetric Skepticism

Evidence supporting a preferred conclusion receives minimal scrutiny.

Evidence challenging the conclusion faces escalating demands.

**Favored claim:**

> “Seems obvious.”

**Counterevidence:**

> “Who funded it?”

> “Has it been replicated?”

> “How do we know those researchers weren't biased?”

Those questions can be legitimate.

The Empiricidal feature is **asymmetry**.

If the same standard would destroy the favored evidence but is never applied to it, skepticism has become strategic rather than epistemic.

---

## 4. Evidential Goalpost Migration

A person requests evidence.

The evidence is supplied.

The requirement changes.

The new requirement is met.

Another requirement appears.

The structure becomes:

**“Show me X.”**

→ X shown.

**“X doesn't count unless Y.”**

→ Y shown.

**“Y doesn't count without Z.”**

The real criterion gradually becomes:

> **No possible evidence may require revision.**

---

## 5. Pseudo-Philosophical Manipulation

Complex philosophical ideas are used as defensive rhetoric rather than tools of inquiry.

Examples can include distorted versions of:

- skepticism
    
- relativism
    
- subjectivity
    
- uncertainty
    
- social construction
    
- fallibilism
    

The concepts themselves are not the problem.

The misuse occurs when they become **universal solvent**, dissolving every inconvenient fact while leaving preferred beliefs untouched.

This connects directly to [[Pseudo-Philosophical Manipulation]].

---

## 6. Authority Substitution

The question:

> **“What evidence supports this?”**

is replaced with:

> **“Who said it?”**

Authority can legitimately alter confidence when expertise is relevant.

Empiricide occurs when authority becomes **immune from evidential correction**, or when disliked authorities are dismissed regardless of evidence while favored authorities are accepted regardless of evidence.

---

## 7. Source Poisoning

Instead of evaluating the information, the observer attempts to make the source epistemically inadmissible.

This may involve:

**source disliked → therefore claim false**

rather than:

**claim examined → evidence insufficient → therefore claim rejected**

A bad source can produce a true statement.

A respected source can produce a false one.

Hyper-analysis separates:

**source credibility**

from

**truth-value of the individual claim**

while using both appropriately.

---

## 8. Selective Empiricism

Empirical standards are invoked only when useful.

> “Show me the evidence.”

when confronting a disliked conclusion.

But:

> “Everybody knows that.”

when defending a preferred one.

This is not empiricism.

It is **evidence as a rhetorical weapon**.

# Relationship to Ignoration

[[Ignoration]] is the broader recursive system.

The database definition describes Ignoration as a self-perpetuating cycle in which a person rejects empiricism, distorts truth, resists correction, and may weaponize relativism or similar strategies against others.

**Empiricidal Tendencies are one of the mechanisms through which that cycle operates.**

A useful distinction is:

**Ignorance**  
→ I do not know.

**Empiricidal Tendency**  
→ Something that could correct me is neutralized.

**Ignoration**  
→ The neutralization becomes recursive, self-protective, and self-perpetuating.

Thus:

> **Ignorance lacks information.**  
> **Empiricide attacks correction.**  
> **Ignoration converts the attack on correction into a stable loop.**

---

# Relationship to Epistemic Cowardice

[[Epistemic Cowardice]] can function as one psychological motive behind Empiricidal Tendencies.

The problem is not simply:

> **“I am wrong.”**

The threatening implication may instead be:

> If this evidence is true, something important about my identity, worldview, morality, group, history, or previous behavior may have to change.

This creates the temptation to attack the evidence rather than metabolize its implications.

The sequence becomes:

**truth threatens identity**

↓

**identity preservation becomes more urgent than truth**

↓

**evidence is neutralized**

↓

**self-concept survives**

In that sense:

> **Epistemic Cowardice fears what the evidence may require.**  
> **Empiricidal Tendencies are methods for preventing the requirement from arriving.**

But motive should not be assumed.

Empiricidal behavior can also arise from habit, social conditioning, poor reasoning, group loyalty, rhetorical strategy, or simple failure to distinguish evidence from interpretation.

---

# Relationship to Belief-Fact Collapse

The Hyper-Sanity framework already identifies the danger of:

> **thinking everything one believes and believing everything one thinks.**

This produces [[Belief-Fact Collapse]].

The boundaries between:

**thought**

**belief**

**inference**

**observation**

**fact**

begin to disappear.

A person may then experience:

> **“I believe X.”**

as psychologically equivalent to:

> **“X is true.”**

Contradictory evidence no longer feels like information.

It feels like an attack.

Empiricidal defenses become more likely because correcting the proposition now threatens the person who has fused with it.

> **When belief becomes fact psychologically, evidence becomes aggression psychologically.**

This helps explain why purely factual correction can sometimes increase defensive certainty rather than reduce it.

---

# Perceptory Empiricide

A related project concept is **Perceptory Assumption**:

> an inferred element supplied by the observer to complete incomplete evidence, which may later be experienced as though it had been directly observed.

The danger is:

**observation**

→ **inference**

→ **assumption**

→ **closure**

→ **assumption remembered as evidence**

An Empiricidal process begins when later evidence contradicting that completion is rejected because the observer no longer remembers which part of the original model came from reality and which part came from themselves.

The epistemic error becomes:

> **My interpretation of the evidence is treated as part of the evidence.**

This can turn an ordinary inference error into a self-sealing belief.

---

# Gaslighting and Interpersonal Empiricide

Empiricidal Tendencies are not synonymous with gaslighting.

They may occur entirely within one person's private reasoning.

But they become especially harmful when used interpersonally.

For example:

**record exists**

↓

**record contradicts preferred narrative**

↓

**record is called meaningless / fake / subjective without adequate reason**

↓

**other person's recollection is attacked**

↓

**preferred narrative is presented as the only legitimate reality**

Here the destruction of empirical standards can become a mechanism of domination.

Instead of merely protecting one's own belief, the person attempts to prevent **another person from using evidence to maintain confidence in their own perception of events**.

This is one reason Empiricidal Tendencies sit naturally inside the Ignoration framework.

# Diagnostic Questions

Before applying the term, ask:

1. **What was actually observed?**
    
2. **What was inferred from the observation?**
    
3. **What part of the conclusion was supplied by interpretation?**
    
4. **What evidence would genuinely change the conclusion?**
    
5. **Is the same evidential standard applied to favored and disfavored claims?**
    
6. **Has the standard changed after evidence met the previous standard?**
    
7. **Is a source being rejected because its methodology failed, or because its conclusion is unwanted?**
    
8. **Is uncertainty being acknowledged, or weaponized to imply that nothing can be known?**
    
9. **Are subjective and empirical claims being deliberately collapsed?**
    
10. **Can the person state the strongest evidence against their own position accurately?**
    
11. **Does the model permit correction in principle?**
    
12. **Is the conclusion being protected, or genuinely tested?**
    

The strongest diagnostic question may be:

> **What observable result would make you conclude that you were wrong?**

If the sincere answer is:

> **“Nothing.”**

then the proposition has largely left empirical reasoning.

# Hyper-Sane Inverse

The inverse of Empiricidal Tendencies is not unquestioning obedience to evidence claims.

It is **Epistemic Courage + Recursive Clarity**.

The Hyper-Sane sequence is:

**evidence appears**

↓

**discomfort is noticed**

↓

**observation is separated from interpretation**

↓

**preferred conclusion is temporarily decentered**

↓

**methods and sources are tested consistently**

↓

**counterevidence is actively considered**

↓

**uncertainty is preserved where necessary**

↓

**belief is revised when warranted**

↓

**the corrected model becomes the new starting point**

This requires [[HS-Epistemic Courage]]:

> **To hold truth even when it threatens identity; to seek reason even when it humbles ego.**

And [[HS-Recursive Clarity]]:

> using each cycle of examination to increase coherence rather than deepen epistemic deadlock.

---

# Recursive Safeguard

Hyper-Sanity itself must remain subject to this concept.

It would be Empiricidal for the framework to claim:

> **“Hyper-Sanity is empirical, therefore evidence against Hyper-Sanity must be wrong.”**

That reverses the entire method.

The correct structure is:

> **Hyper-Sanity attempts to be empirical; therefore evidence against a Hyper-Sanity conclusion must be taken seriously enough to potentially change Hyper-Sanity.**

Empiricism is not loyalty to a set of current facts.

It is loyalty to the **process through which claims remain answerable to reality**.

A framework becomes epistemically dangerous when protecting the framework becomes more important than correcting the framework.

Thus:

> [!warning]+ Recursive Rule  
> **The moment “empirical” becomes an identity that prevents empirical correction, empiricism has become its own opposite.**

The framework must therefore preserve the ability to say:

**I believed this.**

↓

**The evidence changed.**

↓

**I was wrong.**

↓

**The model changes.**

That is not failure.

That is the empirical process functioning correctly.

# Axioms

> **Error is not Empiricidal. Making correction impossible is.**

> **Empiricism asks what evidence does to belief. Empiricide asks what belief must do to evidence in order to survive.**

> **Skepticism seeks stronger evidence. Empiricidal skepticism ensures that inconvenient evidence can never become strong enough.**

> **A belief becomes epistemically dangerous when its survival requires destroying the standards capable of falsifying it.**

> **Belief does not become fact through conviction, and fact does not become belief through disbelief.**

> **When facts become negotiable only when inconvenient, skepticism has become strategy.**

> **An inference should remain distinguishable from the observation that produced it.**

> **The empiricidal mind does not merely reject a fact; it attacks the conditions under which facts are allowed to count.**

> **Empiricism is not loyalty to conclusions. It is loyalty to correction.**

> **A Hyper-Sane framework must remain capable of discovering that it was wrong.**

# Summary

**Empiricidal Tendencies** describe the epistemic transition from merely **being wrong** to developing methods that prevent wrongness from being corrected.

The concept therefore does not condemn ignorance, uncertainty, skepticism, disagreement, or error.

It identifies something more specific:

> **The systematic destruction of evidence's legitimate capacity to alter belief.**

Within Hyper-Sanity, this places Empiricidal Tendencies directly beneath [[Ignoration]] as one of its principal mechanisms.

The deepest distinction is therefore not:

**correct person vs incorrect person**

but:

**correctable cognition vs self-sealing cognition.**

A Hyper-Sane mind can be wrong.

What it cannot safely become is **incapable of discovering that it is wrong.**
