## Green Water

**Definition:**  
Green water is **rainwater** that is stored in the soil and used directly by plants through **evapotranspiration**.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Infiltrates into the ground and is taken up by plant roots.
    
- Critical for agriculture that does not use irrigation (e.g. rain-fed crops).
    
- **Not** directly **visible** or **extractable**-<font color="#4bacc6">unlike blue water</font>.
    

**Relevance to Climate Change:**

- Changes in rainfall patterns and droughts reduce green water availability.
    
- Increased temperatures can raise evapotranspiration, stressing ecosystems.


## Blue Water

**Definition:**  
Blue water refers to **fresh surface and groundwater**-such as rivers, lakes, and aquifers-that is available for **extraction and use**.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Used for irrigation, drinking, industrial processes, and energy production.
    
- Measurable and visible as water bodies or stored groundwater.
    

**Relevance to Climate Change:**

- Droughts and melting glaciers impact blue water availability.
    
- Overuse contributes to water scarcity and groundwater depletion.


## Grey Water

**Definition:**  
Grey water is **wastewater from non-toilet sources** such as sinks, showers, laundry, and kitchen use.

**Key Characteristics:**

- May contain soap, grease, food particles, and low levels of contaminants.
    
- **Can be recycled** after treatment for irrigation or flushing.
    

**Relevance to Climate Change:**

- Grey water reuse reduces freshwater demand.
    
- Helps mitigate pressure on water treatment systems and reduce carbon emissions associated with water pumping and purification.
