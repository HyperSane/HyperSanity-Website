# Cancer

- **Reduced Risk**: Plant-based diets have been linked to a lower risk of various cancers. For instance, a review highlighted that vegetarian diets are associated with a reduced incidence of colorectal cancer, potentially due to higher fiber intake and lower consumption of carcinogenic compounds found in meat ([NMCD Journal](https://www.nmcd-journal.com/article/S0939-4753%2823%2900149-7/fulltext "A comprehensive review of healthy effects of vegetarian diets")).

## NMCD Journal
### Data synthesis

We performed literature research (up to December 31, 2022) of the evidence separately for vascular disease (VD), obesity (OB), dyslipidemia (Dysl), hypertension (HPT), type 2 diabetes (T2D), metabolic syndrome (MetS), analyzing only cohort studies and randomized controlled studies (RCTs) and comparing the effect of V and NV diets. Cohort studies showed advantages of V diets compared to NV diets on incidence and/or mortality risk for ischemic heart disease, overweight and OB risk. Most cohort studies showed V had lower risk of HPT and lower blood pressure (BP) than NV and V diets had positive effects on T2D risk or plasma parameters. The few cohort studies on the risk of MetS reported mixed results. In RCTs, V diets, mainly low-fat-vegan ones, led to greater weight loss and improved glycemic control than NV diets and in the only one RCT a partial regression of coronary atherosclerosis. In most RCTs, V diets significantly reduced LDL-C levels (but also decreased HDL-C levels) and BP.



## 🥩 Red and Processed Meats: Carcinogenic Classifications

* **Processed Meat**: Classified as a Group 1 carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), indicating sufficient evidence that its consumption causes colorectal cancer in humans.([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_meat))

* **Red Meat**: Designated as a Group 2A carcinogen, meaning it is probably carcinogenic to humans, with associations observed primarily for colorectal cancer, but also for pancreatic and prostate cancers.([IARC](https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/pr240_E.pdf))

---

## 📊 Epidemiological Findings

* **Colorectal Cancer**:

  * A systematic review and meta-analysis reported that high consumption of red and processed meats is associated with an increased risk of colorectal, colon, and rectal cancers.([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_and_cancer))

* **Other Cancers**:

  * High red meat intake has been positively associated with increased risks of breast, endometrial, lung, and hepatocellular carcinoma.([SpringerLink](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-021-00741-9))



## 🧬 Biological Mechanisms Linking Meat to Cancer

* **Heme Iron**: Abundant in red meat, heme iron can promote the formation of N-nitroso compounds, which are known carcinogens.([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_meat))

* **Nitrates and Nitrites**: Used in processed meats for preservation, these compounds can convert into carcinogenic nitrosamines in the human body.([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_meat))

* **Cooking Byproducts**: High-temperature cooking methods (e.g., grilling, frying) can produce heterocyclic amines (HCAs) and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), both of which have been linked to cancer.

---

## 🧠 Genetic and Molecular Insights

* **DNA Damage Signatures**: Research has identified specific patterns of DNA damage in colorectal tumors associated with high red meat consumption, suggesting a direct mutagenic effect.([Cancer.gov](https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/cancer-currents-blog/2021/red-meat-colorectal-cancer-genetic-signature))

* **Genetic Susceptibility**: Certain genetic variants may increase an individual's risk of developing colorectal cancer in response to high red or processed meat intake.

---

## 📉 Risk Quantification

* Consuming 50 grams of processed meat daily (approximately one hot dog or a few slices of bacon) increases the risk of colorectal cancer by about 18%.([EatingWell](https://www.eatingwell.com/processed-meats-and-cancer-11728821))

* High intake of red meat is associated with a 30% increased risk of colorectal cancer, while high processed meat consumption is linked to a 40% increased risk.([Keck School of Medicine of USC](https://keck.usc.edu/news/large-scale-study-explores-genetic-link-between-colorectal-cancer-and-meat-intake/))

---

## 🧾 Recommendations

* **Limit Intake**: Health organizations recommend reducing consumption of red and processed meats to lower cancer risk.

* **Dietary Alternatives**: Emphasizing plant-based proteins, such as legumes, nuts, and whole grains, can provide nutritional benefits without the associated cancer risks.

---

These findings underscore the importance of dietary choices in cancer prevention. For personalized dietary advice, consulting with healthcare professionals or registered dietitians is recommended.
